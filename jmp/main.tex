\begin{abstract}
	\noindent
	Using linked household data from seven waves of the China Family Panel
	Studies, I show that upward revisions in inflation expectations predict
	subsequent forecast errors in the opposite direction
	($\hat{\beta}=-0.55$, $N\approx 13{,}000$). The pattern is robust
	across demographic subgroups, alternative expectation codings, and
	progressive controls with province $\times$ wave fixed effects. Aggregate
	depositor-survey data display the same sign. A joint specification
	nesting revision-driven overreaction and lagged-error persistence
	reveals error reversals rather than sluggish updating, though the
	short aggregate sample limits sharp discrimination between models.
	The data display a reversal pattern more consistent with overreaction
	than with a pure sluggish-updating account, though the paper does not
	claim full structural identification of belief formation.
	\\[0.5em]
	\textbf{JEL Codes:} D84, E31, E71, G53 \\
	\textbf{Keywords:} inflation expectations, overreaction,
	household surveys, diagnostic expectations, China
\end{abstract}
