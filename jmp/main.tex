\begin{abstract}
	\noindent
	Do Chinese households overreact to salient inflation signals, and can
	policy exploit that bias? Using seven waves of the China Family Panel
	Studies (CFPS), I estimate a revision--error slope of $\hat{\beta}=-0.55$
	($p<0.001$): households that revise expected inflation upward subsequently
	overshoot realized inflation. Quarterly PBoC depositor-survey data
	corroborate the same sign. A mechanism horse race between diagnostic
	expectations and information rigidity shows both channels matter, with
	diagnostic effects stronger in high-uncertainty episodes. CHFS evidence
	shows a null media amplification effect, implying salience works mainly
	through expectation updating rather than differential media exposure.
	Embedding the estimates in a real-time policy rule reduces
	out-of-sample MAE by 17\% relative to naive survey forecasts. A welfare
	calibration implies gains of 37.6\% from bias-aware communication and
	stabilization policy.
	\\[0.5em]
	\textbf{JEL Codes:} D84, E31, E71, G41 \\
	\textbf{Keywords:} Diagnostic expectations, inflation expectations,
	household surveys, household finance, CHFS, China, salience,
	Bayesian state-space
\end{abstract}
