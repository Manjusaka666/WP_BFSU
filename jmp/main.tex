\begin{abstract}
	\noindent
	Households systematically overpredict inflation after salient food-price shocks. I exploit the 2018--2019 African Swine
	Fever outbreak in China, which reduced the national hog herd by 30--55 percent and generated sharp cross-province
	variation in meat prices. Combining province-level meat CPI data with four waves of the China Family Panel Studies
	(2016--2022), I show that a one-standard-deviation meat-price shock raises household forecast errors by 1.7 percentage
	points, roughly three-quarters of average headline inflation. A signal-extraction model calibrated to the CPI weight of
	meat bounds the rational forecast error at less than one-twentieth of the estimated response. The excess is consistent
	with diagnostic overreaction: households treat a transitory, mean-reverting shock as informative about persistent
	inflation. Placebo tests confirm that grain shocks of comparable CPI weight produce no such pattern. The findings imply
	that salient relative-price movements can durably distort the inflation expectations that anchor consumption and
	wage-setting decisions.
	\\[0.5em]
	\textbf{JEL Codes:} D84, E31, E71 \\
	\textbf{Keywords:} inflation expectations, overreaction,
	diagnostic expectations, salience, household surveys, China
\end{abstract}
