\begin{abstract}
	\noindent
	Do Chinese households overreact to salient inflation signals?
	Using seven waves of the China Family Panel Studies
	($N \approx 13{,}700$), I estimate a revision--error
	coefficient of $\hat{\beta} \approx -0.55$ ($p < 0.001$)
	with province and wave fixed effects: households that
	revise inflation expectations upward subsequently
	overshoot, consistent with diagnostic overreaction.
	Aggregate quarterly data from the PBoC depositor survey
	(2011Q1--2025Q3) corroborate the sign
	($\hat{\beta} = -2.24$, $p = 0.055$, $N = 32$).
	The overreaction is pervasive across income, education,
	and urban/rural groups, with no significant differential
	across demographic terciles. A mechanism-competition
	design nesting diagnostic and information-rigidity
	channels finds both active, with the diagnostic margin
	strengthening during high-uncertainty episodes. Local
	projections show that revision shocks move forecast
	errors within one quarter and then attenuate. A Bayesian
	state-space model tracks time variation in the diagnostic
	coefficient, which intensifies during geopolitical crises
	and pandemic onset. Translating these estimates into a
	real-time forecast-adjustment rule improves out-of-sample
	MAE by 17\% and interval coverage from 42\% to 67\%
	relative to naive survey forecasts.
	\\[0.5em]
	\textbf{JEL Codes:} D84, E31, E71, G41 \\
	\textbf{Keywords:} Diagnostic expectations, inflation expectations,
	household surveys, China, salience, Bayesian state-space
\end{abstract}
