\section{Bayesian State-Space Model}\label{app:ssm}

This appendix details the Bayesian state-space model (SSM)
that tracks how the diagnostic coefficient $\beta_t$
evolves over the sample. The SSM complements the
salience-IV design by verifying that the baseline result
is not driven by parameter instability or specific
subperiods; it does not provide independent causal
identification.

\subsection{Model specification}

The measurement equation links forecast errors to
revisions and controls with a time-varying coefficient:
\begin{align}
  FE_t &= \alpha + \beta_t FR_t + \gamma' Z_t
    + \varepsilon_t, \qquad
    \varepsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, R),
    \label{eq:ssm_measurement} \\
  \beta_t &= \beta_{t-1} + d' X_t + u_t, \qquad
    u_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, Q),
    \label{eq:ssm_state}
\end{align}
where $Z_t$ contains macro controls (food CPI, M2 growth,
PPI inflation), $X_t$ contains EPU and GPR indices as
drivers of time variation, and $R$, $Q$ are scalar
variances for measurement noise and state innovation.

Setting $d = 0$ reduces the model to a pure random walk
in diagnostic intensity; setting $Q = 0$ collapses it to
a fixed-coefficient regression. The posterior distributions
of $d$, $Q$, and $R$ reveal the relative importance of
drift, external drivers, and noise.

\subsection{Prior specification}

I use conjugate priors that encode mild beliefs while
letting the data dominate:
\begin{align*}
  \beta_0 &\sim \mathcal{N}(-0.5, 0.5^2), \\
  \alpha, \gamma &\sim \mathcal{N}(0, 10^2), \\
  d &\sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.2^2), \\
  R &\sim \text{Inv-Gamma}(3, 0.001), \\
  Q &\sim \text{Inv-Gamma}(3, 0.001).
\end{align*}

The prior for $\beta_0$ centers at the OLS point estimate
($-0.5$) with standard deviation $0.5$, permitting the
posterior to range from roughly $-1.5$ to $+0.5$. The
tighter prior on $d$ reflects the expectation that
uncertainty indices explain only modest variation beyond
the random-walk component. Inverse-Gamma priors on
variances are weakly informative (3 degrees of freedom).

\subsection{Estimation}

Posterior inference proceeds via a three-block Gibbs
sampler:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{State path:} Sample $\{\beta_t\}_{t=1}^T$
    conditional on parameters via Forward Filtering
    Backward Sampling \citep{CarterKohn1994}.
  \item \textbf{Regression coefficients:} Sample
    $(\alpha, \gamma, d)$ from conditional normal
    posteriors.
  \item \textbf{Variances:} Sample $R$ and $Q$ from
    conditional Inverse-Gamma posteriors.
\end{enumerate}

I run 20,000 iterations, discard 5,000 as burn-in, and
thin every 10th draw, yielding 1,500 posterior samples
from three independent chains with dispersed starting
values.

\subsection{Convergence diagnostics}

Table~\ref{tab:mcmc_diagnostics} reports convergence
statistics. Geweke $Z$-statistics fall within $\pm 1.5$
for all parameters ($p > 0.05$). Gelman--Rubin $\hat{R}$
values range from 0.9999 to 1.0015, well below the 1.05
threshold. Effective sample sizes range from 411 ($Q$) to
2,665 ($\alpha$), all exceeding the 400 minimum for
reliable inference.

\subsection{Posterior results}

Table~\ref{tab:ssm_posterior_params} reports posterior
means and 90\% credible intervals. The intercept
$\hat{\alpha} = -0.315$ ($[-0.446, -0.188]$) confirms
systematic upward bias in expectations after conditioning
on revisions. Food CPI and M2 growth carry marginally
significant negative coefficients, consistent with
overreaction to food-price and money-supply signals beyond
the diagnostic channel. The central finding is that
$\beta_t$ is negative in 91\% of quarters at the 90\%
credible level.

The drift coefficients are $\hat{d}_{EPU} = +0.067$
($[-0.15, +0.29]$) and $\hat{d}_{GPR} = -0.093$
($[-0.38, +0.19]$). Neither is precisely estimated:
higher policy uncertainty is associated with less negative
$\beta_t$, while geopolitical risk is associated with more
negative $\beta_t$, but the wide intervals caution against
over-interpretation.

\subsection{Prior sensitivity}

Table~\ref{tab:prior_sensitivity} reports posterior results
under three prior configurations: baseline (above), loose
($\sigma_{\beta_0} = 1.0$, $\sigma_d = 0.5$), and tight
($\sigma_{\beta_0} = 0.25$, $\sigma_d = 0.1$). The sign
of $\beta_t$ is negative under all three, and the
posterior mean varies by less than 15\% across priors.
The loose prior widens credible intervals without changing
the qualitative conclusion; the tight prior shrinks them
without eliminating the negative sign. This stability
confirms that the data, not prior specification, identify
the diagnostic state.

\subsection{Model comparison}

Table~\ref{tab:model_comp} compares four specifications:
frequentist OLS with fixed $\beta$, Bayesian SSM with
fixed $\beta$, TVP-SSM without drift, and full TVP-SSM
with EPU/GPR drivers. Both TVP models achieve
substantially higher log-likelihoods than fixed-$\beta$
alternatives ($-26.9$ and $-25.2$ versus $-35.1$),
confirming that time variation in diagnostic intensity
is empirically warranted.
