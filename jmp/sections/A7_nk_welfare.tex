\section{Illustrative New Keynesian Welfare Calibration}\label{app:nk_welfare}

This appendix reports a model-based welfare exercise that
maps the estimated overreaction parameter into a
three-equation New Keynesian benchmark. The exercise is
illustrative: it quantifies the order of magnitude of
welfare costs from diagnostic expectations under standard
calibration, not a policy-invariant welfare mapping.

The model is
\begin{align}
  \pi_t &= \beta E_t^\theta[\pi_{t+1}] + \kappa x_t + u_t, \nonumber \\
  x_t &= E_t[x_{t+1}] - \sigma^{-1}
    \left(i_t - E_t^\theta[\pi_{t+1}]\right), \nonumber \\
  i_t &= \phi_\pi \pi_t + \phi_x x_t, \nonumber
\end{align}
where $E_t^\theta$ denotes diagnostic expectations with
overreaction parameter $\theta$. Calibration uses standard
values: $\beta = 0.99$, $\kappa = 0.05$, $\sigma = 2$,
$\phi_\pi = 1.5$, $\phi_x = 0.5$, and AR(1) cost-push
shocks with persistence $\rho_u = 0.8$.

The welfare loss function is
$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathrm{Var}(\pi) +
\lambda\,\mathrm{Var}(x)$ with
$\lambda = \kappa/\varepsilon \approx 0.0083$.
Table~\ref{tab:nk_welfare} reports welfare losses for a
range of $\theta$ values. At $\theta = 0.55$, aligned
with the household panel estimate, the welfare loss is
approximately 38\% above the rational benchmark
($\theta = 0$). At $\theta = 1.0$, a value closer to
what the aggregate point estimate would imply, the loss
rises to 78\% above benchmark.

The welfare schedule is convex in $\theta$: partial
reductions in overreaction yield first-order gains when
the economy operates in a high-$\theta$ region, even if
communication policy cannot eliminate behavioral
distortions entirely.

Two caveats apply. First, the mapping from a reduced-form
revision--error coefficient to a structural $\theta$
parameter requires assumptions about the data-generating
process that this paper does not formally test. The
calibration is therefore a plausibility check, not a
structural identification result. Second, if policy
communication changes household belief formation, the
reduced-form $\theta$ can shift endogenously (Lucas
critique). The reported numbers are local counterfactuals
around the estimated regime, not invariant policy
constants.
