\section{Robustness}\label{sec:robustness}

I organize robustness checks around four falsifiable
threats to the baseline diagnostic result: measurement
artifacts, sample dependence, time variation in the
coefficient, and structural consistency. Each subsection
targets a specific concern and reports whether the
diagnostic coefficient retains its sign, magnitude, and
significance under the alternative specification.

\subsection{Measurement}

The Carlson--Parkin quantification depends on the
perception threshold $\delta$ and the treatment of
uncertain respondents. Table~\ref{tab:delta_sensitivity}
in Appendix~\ref{app:robustness} reports the diagnostic
coefficient under three alternative threshold values
($\delta \in \{0.3, 0.5, 0.7\}$). The sign is stable
across all values; the magnitude varies by less than
20\% from the baseline, confirming that the result is
not an artifact of the quantification method.

Replacing the Carlson--Parkin measure with the raw PBoC
expectation index, which has a different scale and
measurement error structure, produces the same qualitative
pattern: positive revisions predict negative subsequent
forecast errors (Table~\ref{tab:alt_measures}). Agreement
across two independently constructed expectation measures
provides corroboration that the diagnostic finding reflects
household behavior rather than a quantification artifact.

\subsection{Sample stability}

Dropping the COVID period (2020Q1--2020Q4) attenuates
the coefficient but preserves its sign
(Table~\ref{tab:subsample}). The sign retention confirms
that the result is not driven by pandemic-era outliers,
though significance weakens in the smaller sample as
expected.

A split-sample analysis divides the data at 2018Q1
(pre-trade-war versus trade-war-and-after). Both
subsamples retain the negative diagnostic sign. The
post-2018 estimate is larger in absolute value, consistent
with the heterogeneity results in
Section~\ref{sec:heterogeneity}: diagnostic behavior
intensified during the high-uncertainty period. The
smaller subsample sizes ($N \approx 16$ each) limit
precision, so I treat the sign stability as the
informative margin rather than the point estimates.

\subsection{Time-varying coefficient: Bayesian
  state-space model}

The Bayesian SSM (Appendix~\ref{app:ssm}) provides a
complementary identification strategy by allowing the
diagnostic coefficient to vary each quarter. The key
result is that $\beta_t$ is negative in 91\% of quarters
at the 90\% credible level. Prior sensitivity analysis
shows that the sign and approximate magnitude of
$\beta_t$ are stable across baseline, loose, and tight
prior specifications
(Table~\ref{tab:prior_sensitivity}).

Figure~\ref{fig:beta_t_path} plots the posterior mean
and 90\% credible interval of $\beta_t$ over the sample
period. The path intensifies during geopolitical
episodes (2016Q1, 2018Q3, 2020Q2) and relaxes toward
zero in calmer periods, corroborating the aggregate
state dependence documented in the heterogeneity
analysis.

\subsection{Structural consistency: BVAR}

The BVAR in Appendix~\ref{app:bvar} identifies a
diagnostic-expectations shock using sign restrictions
anchored to the salience proxy rather than circular
definitions. The acceptance rate is 99.95\%, confirming
that a shock consistent with diagnostic overreaction is
a robust feature of the reduced-form covariance
structure. Impulse responses show that the identified
shock raises expectations substantially while realized
inflation barely moves (Figure~\ref{fig:bvar_irf}),
and the implied forecast-error reversal matches the
local-projection evidence from the baseline analysis.
