\section{Institutional Setting and Data}\label{sec:data}

This section describes the three survey datasets and the
quarterly macro controls used in the main specifications.
The CFPS provides the household panel test, the PBoC
depositor survey provides the aggregate quarterly test, and
the CHFS provides cross-sectional mechanism variables on
information channels and risk attitude.

\subsection{CFPS Household Panel}

The China Family Panel Studies (CFPS) is a nationally
representative household panel administered by the Institute
of Social Science Survey at Peking University
\citep{Xie2014,Xie2022}. I use seven waves (2010, 2012,
2014, 2016, 2018, 2020, 2022). The analysis sample for the
baseline household specification contains about 13,700
household-wave observations after requiring non-missing
expectation responses, province identifiers, and panel
linkage.

The CFPS cross-year identifier links respondents across
waves and allows construction of within-household changes in
inflation beliefs. Household-level covariates used in later
sections include age, education, hukou status, and income.
The panel is unbalanced, so all specifications are estimated
on the available linked observations for each wave pair.

The inflation-expectation item is a three-point qualitative
question (prices rise, unchanged, fall). I code
$\mu_{it}\in\{1,0,-1\}$ for household $i$ at wave $t$. For
households observed in consecutive waves, the revision is
\begin{equation*}
\Delta \mu_{it}=\mu_{it}-\mu_{i,t-1}.
\end{equation*}
Forecast errors are constructed by matching each household
to province-level realized CPI inflation between $t$ and
$t+1$, then comparing outcomes with the direction implied by
$\mu_{it}$. This preserves time ordering: revisions use
information at $t$, while errors use realizations after $t$.
The baseline CFPS estimators include province and wave fixed
effects to absorb persistent regional inflation differences
and common macro shocks between survey rounds.

\subsection{PBoC Depositor Survey}

The People's Bank of China Urban Depositor Survey provides
the aggregate expectation series at quarterly frequency.
The working sample in this paper spans 2011Q1--2025Q3.
After merging with macro controls and requiring non-missing
revisions and forecast errors, the baseline aggregate
regressions use $N=32$ quarterly observations
(Table~\ref{tab:sample_overview}).

The survey is fielded quarterly in major cities and reports
aggregate qualitative expectation shares. The estimation
sample is shorter than the raw archive because the baseline
regressions require complete macro controls and consistent
timing for revisions and next-quarter forecast errors.

The expectation question is qualitative and published on a
five-point response scale. I convert the published response
shares into a quantitative expectation using a brief
Carlson--Parkin mapping, with full construction details in
Appendix~\ref{app:data} and Appendix~\ref{app:measurement}. The
quarterly aggregate revision and error are
\begin{equation*}
FR_t=\mu_t-\mu_{t-1},\qquad
FE_{t+1}=\pi_{t+1}^{QoQ}-\mu_t.
\end{equation*}
This timing aligns forecast formation at quarter $t$ with
realized inflation in $t+1$.
Alternative allocations for uncertain responses are retained
for sensitivity analysis; their implications for quantified
expectations are summarized later in
Table~\ref{tab:cp_bounds}.

\subsection{CHFS 2011 Cross-Section}\label{subsec:chfs_data}

The China Household Finance Survey (CHFS) contributes a
cross-sectional mechanism dataset centered on the 2011 wave
\citep{Gan2014}. The key inflation-expectation item is
question A4008, measured on a five-point scale and available
only in 2011. This one-wave availability implies that CHFS
cannot be used to construct within-household expectation
revisions over time.

The 2011 CHFS wave covers approximately 8,400 households and
is the only CHFS wave that includes the A4008 inflation
expectation item used in this paper. Later CHFS waves are
used only for forward linkage outcomes, not for new
expectation revisions.

CHFS 2011 also provides two variables used in mechanism
tests. First, A4001 records six information channels
(newspapers, TV, radio, internet, SMS, family/friends),
which are used to proxy households' exposure to salient
price narratives. Second, A4003 records self-reported risk
attitude on a five-point scale, used as an alternative
behavioral channel.

Although A4008 appears only in 2011, CHFS household
identifiers allow linkage of 2011 beliefs to later waves for
outcome tracking. In linked follow-up analysis, income is
consistently available, while comparable consumption
variables are not available with the same coverage across
waves. For that reason, the forward CHFS consequence
specification uses income outcomes.

The CHFS role in this paper is therefore complementary to
CFPS and PBoC: it identifies mechanism-relevant
cross-sectional variation in information exposure and risk
attitudes, rather than time-series revision dynamics.

\subsection{Macro Controls}

All macro controls are merged at quarterly frequency.
Inflation controls include headline CPI and food CPI from
National Bureau of Statistics releases; food CPI is included
separately because food-price movements are a salient signal
in household inflation perceptions. I also include PPI as an
upstream cost indicator and M2 growth as a monetary
conditions proxy.

All monthly macro series are aligned to the survey quarter
using within-quarter averages. Revisions and errors are then
defined so that controls are dated at $t$ and realized
inflation is dated at $t+1$, matching the forecasting
timeline used in the baseline regressions.

Uncertainty controls are the China Economic Policy
Uncertainty index \citep{Baker2016} and the Geopolitical
Risk index \citep{Caldara2022}. Both are monthly source
series aggregated to quarters by averaging.

The final merged quarterly panel is used in both aggregate
OLS and the directional IV checks. The same macro block is
kept across specifications unless a robustness table states
otherwise.
