\section{Forecasting Application}\label{app:forecasting}

This appendix presents a simple forecasting exercise that exploits the
estimated overreaction pattern. The exercise is illustrative: with only
12 backtest quarters, it demonstrates that the revision-error signal
contains usable forecasting information but cannot establish that the
correction is a reliable variance-minimizing tool.

\subsection{Correction Rule}

The correction is a one-step linear adjustment. Let $FR_t$ denote the
quarter-$t$ inflation forecast revision and $W_t$ collect controls
observed at $t$ (food CPI, M2 growth, PPI). The predicted forecast error
is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:policy_rule_forecasting}
  \widehat{FE}_{t+1 \mid t}
  = \hat{\beta}_{0,t} + \hat{\beta}_{1,t} FR_t + \hat{\gamma}_t' W_t .
\end{equation}
The adjusted inflation forecast is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adjusted_forecast_forecasting}
  \widehat{\pi}^{adj}_{t+1 \mid t}
  = \mu_t + \widehat{FE}_{t+1 \mid t},
\end{equation}
where $\mu_t$ is the raw Carlson--Parkin survey forecast. Under the
estimated sign $\hat{\beta}_{1,t} < 0$, a positive revision implies
subsequent overprediction, so the correction moves the forecast downward.

All inputs are available at decision time. Coefficients are re-estimated
using only information through $t-1$, then frozen for the $t+1$ forecast.

\subsection{Expanding-Window Backtest}

I evaluate the rule over 12 forecast quarters starting from 2011Q1. For
each forecast origin $t$, coefficients are estimated on data through
$t-1$, then used to generate the one-step adjusted forecast for $t+1$.
The benchmark is the raw survey forecast $\mu_t$.

The results show mixed performance. MAE declines by 17\%, from 3.58 to
3.41, and 68\% coverage rises from 42\% to 67\%. RMSE rises from 4.12
to 4.52, reflecting the bias-variance tradeoff: in tail episodes, the
correction overshoots because the subsequent inflation shock does not
mean-revert as quickly as the rule expects.

The adjusted path tracks reversals more closely after large positive
survey revisions, reducing systematic overprediction. It performs less
well in quarters dominated by persistent exogenous shocks.
