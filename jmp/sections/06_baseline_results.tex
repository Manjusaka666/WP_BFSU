\section{Baseline Results}\label{sec:results}

I present results in a strict evidence hierarchy. The CFPS household
panel is the primary result: it delivers the most precise estimate under
the strongest design ($\hat{\beta} \approx -0.55$, significant at 0.1\%,
$N \approx 13{,}000$). The PBoC aggregate series corroborates the sign
in a low-power quarterly sample ($\hat{\beta} = -2.24$, $p = 0.055$,
$N = 32$). The IV exercise provides directional support only, because
first-stage strength is weak ($F = 1.16$). Micro evidence anchors
identification, aggregate evidence checks external consistency, and IV
is interpretive rather than determinative.

\subsection{CFPS Household Panel (Main Result)}
\label{sec:cfps_main_result}

A one-unit upward revision in inflation expectations predicts a
subsequent forecast error 0.55 units lower, holding province $\times$
wave effects fixed. Roughly half of the revision is undone in the
subsequent error. The estimate is significant at 0.1\% and based on
approximately 13,000 household-wave observations, an
order-of-magnitude increase in usable variation relative to the
aggregate time series.

Province $\times$ wave fixed effects absorb everything common to
households observed in the same province and survey wave: the local
inflation environment, province-specific policy changes, national macro
news, and seasonality. The identifying variation is cross-household
differences in revision behavior inside the same province $\times$ wave
cell. Under that design, the negative slope is identified from within-cell
cross-household differences rather than common time-series movements.

The estimate is stable in a progressive-control sense
(Table~\ref{tab:cfps_baseline}). A bivariate specification with only
revisions already delivers $\hat{\beta} = -0.547$. Adding household
demographics yields $\hat{\beta} = -0.550$. Adding province $\times$
wave fixed effects yields $\hat{\beta} = -0.549$. The coefficient is
unchanged across all three columns. This stability limits the scope for
omitted-variable bias: the Oster (2019) bounds analysis in
Section~\ref{sec:identification} shows that unobservables would need to
be over 2,700 times as important as the full set of observables to
drive the coefficient to zero.

The estimate is a reduced-form diagnostic slope, not a structural
parameter pinned down by exogenous treatment timing. But conditional on
the fixed-effects design and observed controls, the estimated slope is
negative relative to both the rational-expectations benchmark
($\beta = 0$) and the canonical rigidity benchmark ($\beta > 0$ in this
setup). Revisions and subsequent errors move in opposite directions with
economically large elasticity.

Heterogeneity results in Section~\ref{sec:heterogeneity} reinforce this
reading. Interactions by education, income, and urban status leave the
base revision slope near $-0.55$ in every case, with no strong
differential slopes. Overreaction is broad-based, not confined to a
narrow subgroup.

\subsection{Aggregate Corroboration}
\label{sec:aggregate_corroboration}

The aggregate exercise asks whether the micro result survives temporal
aggregation. The preferred specification yields
$\hat{\beta} = -2.24$ with $N = 32$ ($p = 0.055$): a one
percentage-point upward revision in aggregate expected inflation predicts
a 2.24 percentage-point forecast error in the opposite direction one
quarter ahead. The sign is consistent with the CFPS evidence.

Across specifications in Table~\ref{tab:ols_baseline}, the bivariate
slope is already negative ($-1.66$). Adding macro controls strengthens
it to $-2.24$. Adding uncertainty controls preserves a large negative
point estimate ($-2.13$). Sign stability across nested columns is the
key discrimination object given the small sample.

Finite-sample inference confirms the direction. The bootstrap $t$-test
yields $p = 0.030$, stronger than the baseline HAC $p$-value. The
percentile confidence interval is wider and includes zero, which is
expected in short samples with skewed finite-sample distributions. The
correct reading is that direction and economic size are stable, while
precision remains bounded by $N = 32$.

I do not over-interpret the aggregate magnitude. With 32 quarterly
observations, the aggregate block corroborates the CFPS evidence on sign
and broad economic magnitude. Additional aggregate diagnostics and control sensitivity
tables are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}.

\subsection{Dynamic Response}
\label{sec:dynamic_response}

Static coefficients summarize average predictive content but do not show
horizon-by-horizon adjustment. I estimate local projections for horizons
$h = 0,1,\ldots,8$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lp}
  y_{t+h} = \alpha_h + \beta_h FR_t + \Gamma_h' W_t + \varepsilon_{t+h},
\end{equation}
where $y_{t+h}$ is either forecast error or realized inflation.

On impact, the forecast-error response is $\hat{\beta}_0 = -2.24$
(SE $= 1.11$), matching the baseline OLS by construction. At longer
horizons, the path alternates in sign. The impact sign is unambiguously
negative. The oscillation arrives as the effective sample shrinks with
horizon ($N$ declines from 32 at $h = 0$ to 23 at $h = 8$), so
medium-horizon coefficients should be read as suggestive rather than
precise.

The inflation-response LP complements this pattern. Realized inflation
reacts positively on impact, consistent with revisions containing
genuine news, but the forecast-error response is still negative. That
combination is consistent with ``news plus overreaction'': agents
update in the right direction yet overshoot in magnitude. The dynamic
profiles show that the effect is front-loaded: most economically
relevant action occurs at impact, and later coefficients fluctuate
around a less stable trajectory.

\subsection{IV Directional Check}
\label{sec:iv}

First-stage strength is weak ($F = 1.16$), so 2SLS magnitudes are not
reliable for structural interpretation. The Anderson--Rubin 95\%
confidence set is wide ($[-3,3]$), and the reduced-form coefficient is
negative ($-0.41$), matching the direction of the CFPS and aggregate OLS
results.

IV evidence is directionally consistent with diagnostic overreaction, but
it cannot rank plausible magnitudes. The paper's quantitative conclusions
rest on CFPS plus aggregate corroboration, not on IV point estimates.
Detailed IV implementation and exclusion diagnostics appear in
Section~\ref{sec:identification} and Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}.
