\section{Empirical Strategy}\label{sec:identification}

The CFPS household panel is the main empirical object in this paper. It
delivers the identifying variation, the sharpest precision, and the most
transparent economic interpretation. The aggregate OLS specification on the
PBoC quarterly series is a sign consistency check. The IV exercise is
directional corroboration only; its first stage is weak, so it is
not informative for magnitude.

\subsection{CFPS Panel Design: Core Identification}

I estimate the revision--error relationship at the household level using
linked CFPS observations across consecutive survey waves:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cfps_panel}
  \text{Error}_{ipt}
  = \alpha + \beta \,\text{Revision}_{ipt}
  + \Gamma' X_{ipt} + \delta_{pt} + \varepsilon_{ipt},
\end{equation}
where $\text{Error}_{ipt}$ is household $i$'s inflation forecast error in
province $p$ and wave $t$, $\text{Revision}_{ipt}$ is the inter-wave
change in expected inflation, $X_{ipt}$ contains household controls, and
$\delta_{pt}$ are province $\times$ wave fixed effects. Standard errors are
clustered at the province level.

The province $\times$ wave fixed effects are the central design feature. They
absorb everything common to households observed in the same province and
the same survey wave: the local inflation level, any province-specific
policy change, nationwide macro news, and seasonality. After conditioning
on $\delta_{pt}$, the remaining variation is cross-household differential
updating within a cell. Two households in Guangdong in the 2014 wave face
the same realized inflation, the same food prices, and the same monetary
policy stance. They differ only in how much they revised their inflation
expectations between waves. The estimand is therefore a reduced-form
relation between relative updating intensity and subsequent forecast error,
conditional on local conditions.

This estimand does not identify a deep structural parameter of a
belief-formation model. It does identify economically meaningful
overreaction in beliefs. Under rational expectations, $\beta$ should be
zero: conditional on the same information environment, larger revisions
should not predict larger errors in the opposite direction. Under
diagnostic expectations, $\beta < 0$: households that revise more
aggressively overshoot more. The estimate is $\hat{\beta} = -0.55$ with
$p < 0.001$, rejecting the rational benchmark.

\paragraph{Addressing the selection concern.}
Households in the same province $\times$ wave cell may differ in unobserved
pessimism, financial literacy, attention, or interpretation thresholds.
If such traits both raise revisions and independently worsen forecast
errors, the OLS coefficient would be biased. I address this concern with
\citet{Oster2019} bounds. The implied proportional-selection threshold is
$\delta^{*} = -2{,}737$: unobservables would need to be over 2,700 times
as important as the full set of observables, and oppositely signed, to
drive the coefficient to zero. The bias-adjusted estimate under $\delta=1$
is $-0.566$, essentially unchanged from the na\"ive estimate. This makes
it implausible that unobserved heterogeneity accounts for the result.

\paragraph{Sample construction.}
The raw CFPS panel contains about 80,000 person-wave observations. Linking
respondents across adjacent waves with valid expectation information yields
roughly 30,000 linked observations. Imposing the full regression
requirements for revision and error proxies yields the estimation sample of
about 13.7k household-wave observations.
Appendix~\ref{app:data} documents these filters in detail.

\subsection{Aggregate OLS as a Sign Check}

I also estimate the revision--error regression in the quarterly PBoC
series ($N=32$) with Newey--West standard errors:
\begin{equation*}
  FE_{t+1}
  = \alpha + \beta \Delta \mu_t + \Psi'W_t + \varepsilon_t,
\end{equation*}
where $\Delta \mu_t$ is the quarterly revision in expected inflation and
$W_t$ includes baseline macro controls. Under rational expectations
$\beta = 0$; under diagnostic overreaction $\beta < 0$. The aggregate
estimate is negative, matching the CFPS sign. I use this specification as
a consistency check on direction, not as a source of precise magnitudes.
A 32-quarter sample has limited statistical power, and aggregate
time-series regressions face the standard concern that common shocks move
both revisions and realized inflation simultaneously.

\subsection{IV as Directional Corroboration}

As supplementary evidence, I instrument attention-driven revision pressure
with media congestion, defined by the negative standardized
geopolitical-risk series $-z(GPR_t)$. When geopolitical news intensity
rises, media bandwidth for inflation coverage falls, shifting salience
without requiring a direct domestic inflation innovation. The first-stage
F-statistic is 1.16, well below conventional thresholds. The
Anderson--Rubin confidence interval is wide ($[-3,3]$), and 2SLS
magnitudes are therefore not informative for the size of overreaction. The
reduced form is negative ($-0.41$), directionally consistent with
diagnostic overreaction. I use the IV only as corroboration of the sign,
not for point identification. Full construction of the MSI index, the
three-equation IV chain, the first-stage figure, and placebo outputs
appear in the online appendix.

\subsection{Threats to Identification}

\paragraph{Commodity-price channels.}
Geopolitical shocks can move oil, imported-input costs, and exchange-rate
pass-through, creating a direct path from $GPR$ to inflation outcomes.
The core macro controls (food CPI, PPI inflation, M2 growth) absorb the
most immediate price-level transmission channels. The identifying claim
does not require zero pass-through, only that remaining variation is
predominantly informational.

\paragraph{Broad macro shocks.}
If the instrument captured generalized downturn risk rather than attention
reallocation, it should predict non-inflation outcomes with similar
strength. Appendix placebo regressions do not show stable non-inflation
effects, consistent with a salience channel rather than a generic macro
shock. This is why I treat the IV as a directional check rather than as a
source of treatment-effect magnitude.

\paragraph{Anticipation.}
If households revise before the measured event window, timing is
contaminated. Lead-based placebo timing tests in the appendix are close to
zero, supporting the maintained sequencing from revision to subsequent
error. The timing evidence is not definitive but rejects the strongest
version of pre-trend-driven reverse causality.

\paragraph{Finite-sample limits.}
All aggregate exercises rest on 32 quarters, and IV precision is further
limited by weak first-stage variation. The empirical claim is based on the
high-power within-cell CFPS panel estimates. Aggregate and IV evidence is
corroborative. Causal language in this paper is anchored to the household
panel design.
