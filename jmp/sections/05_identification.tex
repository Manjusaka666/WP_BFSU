\section{Empirical Strategy}\label{sec:identification}

This section separates identification from corroboration. The main
estimand is the revision--error coefficient under diagnostic
expectations, where $\beta < 0$ implies that upward revisions
systematically overshoot subsequent inflation. I treat the CFPS panel as
the core identification design, use aggregate OLS as a sign-consistency
check, and use the IV only as directional corroboration because the
first stage is weak.

\subsection{CFPS Panel Design}

The primary design uses linked CFPS household observations across
consecutive survey waves. I estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cfps_panel}
  \text{Error}_{ipt}
  = \alpha + \beta \,\text{Revision}_{ipt}
  + \Gamma' X_{ipt} + \delta_{pt} + \varepsilon_{ipt},
\end{equation}
where $\text{Error}_{ipt}$ is household $i$'s inflation forecast error in
province $p$ and wave $t$, $\text{Revision}_{ipt}$ is the inter-wave
change in expected inflation, $X_{ipt}$ contains household controls, and
$\delta_{pt}$ are province-wave fixed effects. Standard errors are
clustered at the province level. Because $\delta_{pt}$ absorbs all shocks
shared within a province-wave cell, identification comes from within-cell
differences in revision behavior across households.

This design directly addresses the central confound in aggregate
time-series specifications: common shocks that simultaneously move
revisions and realized inflation. The identifying comparison is between
households observed in the same province and the same survey wave, so any
province-specific inflation level, local policy change, or nationwide
macro news that is common within that cell is differenced out. Under
diagnostic expectations, the prediction is $\beta < 0$. The estimate is
$\hat{\beta} = -0.55$ with $p < 0.001$, consistent with economically
large overreaction and substantially tighter precision than the aggregate
series.

The within-cell estimand is also transparent economically: conditional on
the same local inflation environment in a given wave, households that
revise expectations more aggressively subsequently make larger forecast
errors in the opposite direction. This interpretation does not rely on a
dynamic filtering model or a latent-state decomposition. It is a direct
reduced-form test of whether stronger belief updating predicts larger
ex-post miss rates after conditioning on rich location-time effects.

To assess omitted-variable bias, I report \citet{Oster2019} bounds for
the CFPS coefficient. The implied proportional-selection threshold is
$\delta^{*} = -1334$, and the bias-adjusted estimate under $\delta = 1$
is $-0.562$. Both metrics indicate that unobservables would need to be
implausibly strong and oppositely signed relative to observables to drive
the coefficient to zero.

Sample construction proceeds in three steps. The raw CFPS panel contains
about 80,000 person-wave observations. Linking respondents across adjacent
waves with valid expectation information yields roughly 30,000 linked
observations. Imposing the full regression requirements for revision and
error proxies yields the estimation sample of about 13.7k
household-wave observations. Appendix~\ref{app:data} documents these
filters in detail.

\subsection{Aggregate OLS}

I also estimate the aggregate revision--error regression in the quarterly
PBoC series ($N=32$), with Newey--West standard errors to account for
serial correlation and heteroskedasticity. This specification is not used
as stand-alone causal evidence; it is a sign-consistency check against the
micro design. The aggregate coefficient remains negative, matching the
CFPS sign and supporting the same model discrimination logic, while
acknowledging the low-power nature of a 32-quarter sample.

The aggregate specification is
\begin{equation*}
  FE_{t+1}
  = \alpha + \beta \Delta \mu_t + \Psi'W_t + \varepsilon_t,
\end{equation*}
where $\Delta \mu_t$ is the quarterly revision in expected inflation and
$W_t$ includes the baseline macro controls. Under rational expectations,
the conditional $\beta$ should be near zero; under diagnostic
overreaction, it should be negative. The aggregate estimate is therefore
interpreted as a discipline check on direction, not as a source of precise
magnitudes.

\subsection{IV as Corroboration}

As supplementary evidence, I instrument attention-driven revision
pressure with media congestion, defined by the negative standardized
geopolitical-risk series, $-z(GPR_t)$: when geopolitical news intensity
rises, media bandwidth for inflation coverage falls, shifting salience
without requiring a direct domestic inflation innovation. In the data,
the first-stage F-statistic is 1.16, so the design is weak-instrument by
conventional standards; the Anderson--Rubin confidence interval is wide
($[-3,3]$), and 2SLS magnitudes are therefore not informative. The
reduced form is negative ($-0.41$), which is directionally consistent
with the diagnostic mechanism, but the IV is used only as corroboration.
Full construction of the MSI index, the three-equation IV chain, the
first-stage figure, and placebo outputs is reported in
the online appendix.

\subsection{Threats to Identification}

\paragraph{Commodity-price channels.}
Geopolitical shocks can move oil, imported-input costs, and exchange-rate
pass-through, creating a direct path from $GPR$ to inflation outcomes.
This risk is mitigated by including the core macro controls used
throughout (food CPI, PPI inflation, and M2 growth), which absorb the
most immediate price-level transmission channels.
The identifying claim therefore does not rely on a zero pass-through
assumption, only on remaining variation being predominantly informational.

\paragraph{Broad macro shocks.}
If the instrument captured generalized downturn risk rather than attention
reallocation, it should predict non-inflation outcomes with similar
strength. Appendix placebo regressions do not show stable non-inflation
effects, which is more consistent with a salience channel than with a
generic macro-shock channel.
This is why I interpret the IV as a directional mechanism check instead of
as a stand-alone source of treatment-effect magnitude.

\paragraph{Anticipation.}
An anticipation concern is that households revise before the measured
event window, contaminating timing. Lead-based placebo timing tests are
close to zero in the appendix, which supports the maintained sequencing
assumption from revision to subsequent error.
The timing evidence is not definitive, but it rejects the strongest
version of pre-trend-driven reverse causality.

\paragraph{Finite-sample limits.}
All aggregate exercises are based on 32 quarters, and IV precision is
further limited by weak first-stage variation. I therefore do not use the
IV for point identification. The empirical claim is based on consistency
across designs, with identification weight assigned to the high-power
within-cell CFPS panel estimates.
In practice, this means the paper's causal language is anchored to the
panel design and treats aggregate-IV evidence as corroborative.
