\section{Conceptual Framework}\label{sec:literature}

Two frictions can distort household inflation expectations: diagnostic
overreaction and information rigidity. Diagnostic expectations follow
representativeness. Agents overweight states made vivid by recent news and
underweight base rates \citep{Kahneman1972, Gennaioli2010}. In macro
applications, revisions overshoot and then partially reverse
\citep{Bordalo2018, Bordalo2020b}. Information rigidity imposes a different
friction. Households update slowly because acquiring and processing information
is costly \citep{Sims2003, Coibion2015}. Errors decay gradually rather than
reversing.

I estimate both channels jointly and use sign restrictions to separate them.
The question is which channel accounts for more forecast-error variation in a
setting with high food-price salience and relatively opaque policy
communication.

\subsection{Diagnostic vs rigidity predictions}\label{sec:diagnostic_vs_rigidity}

Define the forecast error and forecast revision as
\begin{equation*}
  FE_{t+1} \equiv \pi_{t+1} - \mu_t, \qquad FR_t \equiv \mu_t - \mu_{t-1},
\end{equation*}
where $\pi_{t+1}$ is realized inflation and $\mu_t$ is the expectation formed
at time $t$. I estimate a nested specification:
\begin{equation*}
  FE_{t+1} = \alpha + \beta FR_t + \lambda FE_t + X_t'\Gamma + u_{t+1}.
\end{equation*}
The coefficient $\beta$ captures revision overshoot. The coefficient $\lambda$
captures persistence in inherited forecast gaps.

Under diagnostic expectations, upward revisions are too large relative to
subsequent realizations. A positive revision is followed by a negative future
error:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diagnostic_prediction}
  \beta < 0 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \E[FE_{t+1}\mid FR_t>0] < 0.
\end{equation}
This is the overreaction moment in \citet{Bordalo2020b}. It implies an
overshoot-and-correction profile.

Under information rigidity, households close information gaps slowly. Errors
persist because updating is incomplete:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rigidity_prediction}
  \lambda > 0 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \E[FE_{t+1}\mid FE_t>0] > 0.
\end{equation}
This is the sluggish-updating moment in \citet{Coibion2015}. Adjustment is
monotone rather than reversal-based.

The two channels can coexist. A household may underreact to low-salience
background information and overreact when a salient shock dominates attention.
In that case, $\beta<0$ and $\lambda>0$ can hold in the same sample. Joint
estimation is therefore necessary because separate regressions can misattribute
one channel to the other.

\subsection{Chinese institutional context}\label{sec:china_context}

China provides conditions under which both channels can be strong. On the
salience side, food inflation is visible in daily shopping, and pork prices
are the primary amplifier of household inflation narratives. African Swine
Fever episodes created abrupt supply losses and large price spikes. When
households observe these spikes repeatedly, recent food news becomes a dominant
signal for aggregate inflation. This pushes behavior toward representativeness.

On the rigidity side, households face a communication environment with less
explicit forward guidance and less transparent policy mapping than benchmark
inflation-targeting regimes \citep{Chen2022}. When households cannot map policy
messages into a clear inflation path, they update with delay. That delay raises
forecast-error persistence, the signature captured by $\lambda>0$.

Policy implications differ by mechanism. If diagnostic overreaction dominates,
communication should counter salience cascades and anchor expectations around
medium-run fundamentals. If rigidity dominates, policy should improve
frequency, clarity, and predictability of communication to reduce the stock of
uninformed households. Both channels favor better communication, but the
optimal design differs.

\subsection{Testable predictions summary}\label{sec:testable_predictions}

The design evaluates four empirical moments the data can speak to.
The revision-error sign: diagnostic models predict $\beta<0$, with
larger upward revisions preceding more negative forecast errors
\citep{Bordalo2020b}; pure rigidity does not require this overshoot
moment.
The lagged-error sign: rigidity predicts $\lambda>0$, with positive
errors today carrying into next-period errors \citep{Coibion2015};
diagnostic models allow weak or negative lagged-error dependence when
correction follows overshoot.
Cross-sectional media patterns: diagnostic models predict stronger
overreaction where inflation news is more salient; rigidity models
predict faster updating where information access is better, implying
weaker persistence in those groups.
Dynamic patterns: diagnostic dynamics are front-loaded, with revision
shocks moving errors quickly then attenuating or reversing; rigidity
dynamics are gradual, with errors decaying slowly and same-sign
persistence across adjacent horizons. These profiles provide a second
discriminating margin beyond static sign restrictions.

I do not claim these moments deliver a decisive structural test between the two
models. The paper documents patterns consistent with overreaction and quantifies
how much each channel contributes to forecast-error variation in Chinese
household data.
