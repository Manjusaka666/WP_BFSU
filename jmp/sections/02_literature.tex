\section{Conceptual Framework and Related Literature}\label{sec:literature}

Diagnostic expectations rest on representativeness:
agents overweight states whose likelihood has recently
increased, because vivid or surprising news comes to mind
more readily than base rates \citep{Kahneman1972}.
\citet{Gennaioli2010} formalize the intuition with a
multiplicative distortion governed by a single parameter
$\theta$ that indexes diagnostic intensity.
\citet{Bordalo2018} show that this mechanism generates
predictable boom-bust cycles in credit spreads, and
\citet{Bordalo2020b} extend the test to macroeconomic
expectations, documenting that consensus forecast revisions
predict subsequent errors with a negative sign across
multiple variables. This paper takes the
\citet{Bordalo2020b} diagnostic model as the maintained
hypothesis and asks whether its testable predictions
survive in a setting where information frictions are larger,
food-price salience is more volatile, and institutional
communication is less transparent than in U.S. surveys.

\subsection{Diagnostic overreaction versus information rigidity}\label{sub:diagnostic_vs_rigidity}

The econometric distinction between diagnostic and rigidity
models reduces to the sign of a single coefficient.
Define the one-step-ahead forecast error as
$FE_{t+1} = \pi_{t+1} - \mu_t$ and the revision as
$FR_t = \mu_t - \mu_{t-1}$. The diagnostic prediction is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diagnostic_prediction}
  \E[FE_{t+1} \mid FR_t > 0] < 0,
\end{equation}
stating that upward revisions are followed by negative
errors because the revision overshoots the warranted
Bayesian update. The information-rigidity prediction,
following \citet{Coibion2015}, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rigidity_prediction}
  \E[FE_{t+1} \mid FE_t > 0] > 0,
\end{equation}
reflecting persistent undershoot of the full-information
rational expectation. Under rational expectations, revisions
and subsequent errors are orthogonal.

These predictions need not be mutually exclusive: a
household could underreact to some signals and overreact
to others depending on salience and attention allocation
\citep{Sims2003, Mackowiak2009}. The empirical design in
this paper tests both channels in the same specification, so
that neither wins by omission of the other. What matters for
mechanism discrimination is which channel dominates
conditional on the other and whether that dominant channel
exhibits the state dependence its theory predicts.

Most existing tests use professional forecaster panels where
approximate rationality is more defensible and long histories
provide statistical power \citep{Bordalo2020b, Mankiw2002}.
The extension to household surveys is less developed because
household expectations are noisier, reflecting heterogeneous
information sets, and surveys are often qualitative or low
frequency \citep{Mankiw2003}. These measurement challenges
change the identification burden rather than invalidate the
test. With noisier data, OLS attenuation biases the
diagnostic coefficient toward zero, making a significant
negative estimate more rather than less informative. The
Carlson--Parkin quantification introduces measurement error
whose direction depends on the sensitivity parameter
$\delta$. I handle this through bound analysis: allocating
uncertain responses to pessimistic and optimistic extremes
generates bounds on the quantified expectation, and the
diagnostic sign must hold across bounds to be credible.

\subsection{Chinese institutional context}\label{sub:china_context}

China's institutional environment amplifies both channels.
Food-price volatility far exceeds OECD norms because
pork-cycle dynamics, weather shocks, and African Swine Fever
outbreaks generate large, sudden price movements that
saturate media coverage and everyday conversation. When pork
prices rose over 100\% year-on-year in late 2019, the
salience of inflation was impossible for any urban household
to ignore. This is exactly the environment where
representativeness should dominate base-rate reasoning: a
vivid, recent price signal that is easy to recall and hard to
calibrate against historical distributions.

On the rigidity side, the PBoC's communication
infrastructure is less developed than the Federal Reserve's
or the ECB's. Forward guidance is limited, press conferences
are infrequent, and the monetary-policy framework has shifted
multiple times between quantity-based and price-based targets
during the sample period \citep{Chen2022}. Information
asymmetry between the central bank and households may
therefore be larger in China, generating sticky updating even
absent cognitive biases. \citet{Gabaix2020} shows that
bounded rationality in expectations can resolve several macro
puzzles including the forward-guidance puzzle
\citep{Angeletos2018}; China's opaque communication regime
makes such bounded rationality especially plausible.

The policy stakes differ across channels. If diagnostic
overreaction dominates, communication should counteract vivid
narratives rather than simply releasing more information. If
rigidity dominates, more frequent and clearer communication
will reduce errors by closing the information gap. These are
different prescriptions for the same central bank, and
distinguishing between them requires an identification
strategy that separates salience from information provision.

\subsection{Household heterogeneity and attention}\label{sub:heterogeneity}

Diagnostic behavior should vary across households. Attention
is scarce, and households with lower education, less
financial literacy, or tighter cognitive budgets rely more
heavily on the most salient signal rather than integrating
multiple sources \citep{Lusardi2014, Reis2006}.
\citet{Malmendier2016} show that experienced inflation shapes
beliefs persistently, with stronger effects among demographics
that process statistical information less fluently.
\citet{DAutume2024} document greater dispersion in inflation
expectations among less-educated respondents in both U.S. and
European surveys. \citet{Mankiw2003} find substantial
cross-sectional disagreement inconsistent with homogeneous
rational updating, consistent with heterogeneous attention
allocation under rational inattention \citep{Sims2003,
Mackowiak2009}.

The CFPS microdata allow a direct test of this heterogeneity
prediction. By interacting the revision--error slope with
household characteristics---education, income, urban/rural
registration, financial-asset holdings---I test whether
diagnostic intensity is monotonically increasing in proxies
for attention constraints. This test goes beyond aggregate
state dependence because it uses cross-sectional variation
within a given information environment rather than comparing
macro states that may confound attention with other
time-varying factors. Individual-level heterogeneity in
diagnostic intensity can discipline the attention parameters
that theoretical models treat as free.
